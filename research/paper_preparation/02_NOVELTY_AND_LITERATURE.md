# Novelty assessment and additional literature

## Assessment

**Plausible modest empirical novelty; not yet established as a unique contribution.** Use the joint, controlled application to U.S. HY spreads as the proposed contribution. Do not claim that regime forecasting, onset labels, or aggregation-aware baselines were invented here.

The additional search on 25 September 2026 used combinations of “credit spread forecasting temporal aggregation persistence regime transition early warning benchmark,” “credit spreads temporal aggregation forecast,” and exact-title searches. Primary publisher, author and central-bank sources are linked below. Search coverage is incomplete; the earlier review records unsuccessful direct Scholar/Semantic Scholar access. An unsuccessful search is not proof of absence.

## Claim ladder

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| “First regime-based credit-spread forecasting model” | Unsupported and contradicted by prior work |
| “A new last-daily benchmark for monthly data” | Incorrect as a general novelty claim |
| “A new definition of stress onset” | Unsupported; use established event-forecasting concepts |
| “A controlled empirical assessment of aggregation, persistence and incremental macro regimes in this U.S. HY dataset” | Defensible description of the proposed study |
| “These controls substantially change measured regime value” | Testable contribution; matched experiments pending |
| “Regime gains generalize beyond this sample” | Requires external validation; currently unsupported |

Suggested contribution sentence: “We evaluate the incremental predictive value of macroeconomic regimes for monthly U.S. high-yield spreads under matched information sets, explicitly separating aggregation-related forecast gains from stress-entry performance.”

Before submission, inspect the closest full papers for target frequency, latest-spread baselines, release/vintage treatment, regime fitting, at-risk populations, and dependent-data inference. Record “unverified” when unavailable. Backward/forward citation chasing around the aggregation papers and Li–Xiao should precede any “first” or “previously unexplored” wording. Do not infer other authors' implementation errors from absent details.
